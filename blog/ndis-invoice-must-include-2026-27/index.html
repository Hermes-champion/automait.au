What an NDIS invoice must include (2026–27)

By AUTOMAIT · 19 November 2026

An NDIS invoice needs to show who you are (business name and ABN), who you supported (name and NDIS number), what you delivered (support item number, dates, quantity), the price and total, and the GST position. Where it goes depends on how the participant manages their funding.

Checked against National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) and Australian Taxation Office (ATO) pages on 18 November 2026. The pricing schedule that applies is the 2026–27 one (NDIA).

General information only, not legal, tax or NDIS advice. AUTOMAIT is not affiliated with, endorsed by or registered with the NDIA or the NDIS. For your situation, check with the NDIA, the NDIS Commission, a registered tax agent or the ATO.

The fields an NDIS invoice needs

The NDIA's minimum for every provider record is the participant's name, NDIS number, the date(s) the support was delivered, the amount and quantity or hours delivered, and the support type. Invoices need those plus the items below (NDIA record keeping).

Our own suggestions, not NDIA requirements: an invoice number, an invoice date, your bank details and a due date. Plan managers can ask for extras, so get their invoicing rules before your first shift.

What price can go on the invoice?

The Minister's pricing determination sets maximum prices for 2026–27. The NDIA says it can't pay a claim above the maximum and providers can't charge more than it (NDIA pricing arrangements). The current schedule is effective 24 September 2026. The limits apply to NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants only, and you can agree a lower price with any participant (NDIA).

Four price limits rise for support delivered on or after 1 December 2026: house or yard maintenance, house cleaning and other household activities, assistance with personal domestic activities, and specialised home-based assistance for a child. Use the maximum price that applied on the date the support was delivered. Changing an existing service agreement needs the participant's agreement first (NDIA news).

The GST line: tax invoice or not?

Start with whether you're registered for GST. For most businesses, the GST registration threshold is $75,000. Check both your current GST turnover (the current month and previous 11 months) and your projected GST turnover (the current month and next 11 months). If your projected GST turnover is $75,000 or more, you must register. If your current GST turnover is at or above $75,000 but your projected GST turnover will be below it, the ATO says you do not have to register on that turnover basis. Once you're required to register, you must do so within 21 days. Special registration rules can apply, so check the ATO's registration guidance or a registered tax agent for your situation. If you're not registered, you don't charge GST, the GST line says so, and the ATO says your invoice should not use the words "tax invoice" (ATO).

If you are registered, the ATO says a supply to an NDIS participant is GST-free when all of these hold: the participant has an NDIS plan in effect; the supply is of reasonable and necessary supports specified in the plan; there is a written agreement between you and the participant (or another person, such as a plan manager); and the supply is covered by the NDIS GST-free determination, which applies to supplies made before 1 July 2027 (ATO). If one condition fails, the supply may be taxable.

So there are two cases. For a GST-free supply, show the amount and note that it's GST-free. For a taxable supply, the ATO's tax invoice rules apply. For sales under $1,000 the invoice must show that it's intended as a tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a brief description with quantity and price, the GST amount, and the extent to which each item is taxable. From $1,000 it must also show the buyer's identity or ABN. An invoice with taxable and non-taxable items must show which are taxable (ATO tax invoices). If your supplies are mixed, ask a registered tax or BAS agent.

Why NDIS claims get rejected

The NDIA's troubleshooting page lists the usual causes (NDIA):

  • wrong dates, price or funding management type
  • a duplicate claim
  • missing bank or ABN details
  • a unit price above the maximum price
  • for NDIA-managed funding, not being recorded as a my provider

Its checklist also asks whether the budget still has funding, whether the claim dates fall within the correct plan, and whether the claim fits the pricing schedule. Claims later than 90 days can be held for review for up to 28 days, and payment requests must be made within 2 years (NDIA).

Checklist before you send

  • Your business name and ABN are on it.
  • The participant's name, NDIS number and address are right, and it covers one participant only.
  • Each line has the support item number, date and quantity.
  • Each unit price is at or under the 2026–27 maximum for that item (plan-managed and NDIA-managed).
  • The total adds up and the GST line matches your registration and the ATO's conditions.
  • You've checked the funding type, the remaining budget and that the dates fall within the plan.
  • You hold the records behind it: support log, case notes, service agreement.

Frequently asked questions

What must an NDIS invoice include?

Your business name and ABN, the participant's name and NDIS number and address, the date or dates of support, the quantity or hours, the support type and support item number, the claim type, the total, and the GST component if any applies. Plan managers also need your ABN on the invoice. One invoice covers one participant only. That is the NDIA's list for providers; check it against your service agreement and your plan manager's requirements.

Is an NDIS invoice a tax invoice?

Only if you're registered for GST and the supply is taxable. Many NDIS supports are GST-free when the ATO's conditions are met. If you aren't registered for GST, you don't charge it, and the ATO says your invoice should not use the words "tax invoice". If you are registered and a supply is taxable, the ATO's tax invoice rules apply to that supply.

Do NDIS price limits apply to self-managed participants?

No. The NDIA says the maximum prices in its pricing schedules only apply to NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants. For a self-managed participant you agree the price with them, and it's sensible to record it in your service agreement.

How long do I have to claim an NDIS payment?

The NDIA says payment requests must be made within 2 years after the support is delivered. For NDIA-managed claims, submit within 90 days to avoid delays. Claims after 90 days may be held for review, which can take up to 28 days.

Why was my NDIS claim rejected?

The NDIA lists incorrect information such as the date, price or funding management type, a duplicate claim, missing bank or ABN details, and a unit price above the maximum. For NDIA-managed funding, not being recorded as a my provider can also cause rejection. The portal's error message tells you which one applies.
