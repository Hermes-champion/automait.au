Payday Super from 1 July 2026: what small employers in Australia need to do

By AUTOMAIT · 16 November 2026

If you've got a few people on the books, super used to be a quarterly job. That changed on 1 July 2026. Under Payday Super you pay your employees' super guarantee for each payday, not each quarter (ATO).

Here's what changed, the dates, and a checklist. Facts checked on ato.gov.au on 11 November 2026.

General information only, not tax or financial advice. Payday Super has exceptions this article doesn't cover. Check the ATO's Payday Super pages, or talk to a registered tax or BAS agent or contact the ATO about your situation.

The short version

  • Payday Super applies to earnings you pay from 1 July 2026.
  • Super must reach your employee's fund within 7 business days after payday (longer in some cases, such as a new employee).
  • Super is still 12%, now of "qualifying earnings".
  • The last quarterly payment (April to June 2026) was due 28 July 2026.
  • The ATO's Small Business Superannuation Clearing House has closed.

The 7 business day rule

The ATO says your contribution is on time "if it is received by your employee's super fund (with all the necessary information to allocate the contribution to the employee's member account) within 7 business days after paying your employee" (ATO payment deadlines). The clock starts on what the ATO calls the QE day, the day you pay qualifying earnings. For most businesses that's payday.

Two things catch people out. First, received means received. In the ATO's words, "A payment only counts once it is received by the employee's fund, not when it is submitted." Second, a fund that can't match a payment has 3 business days to send it back, and you don't get extra time to fix the error (ATO). That's why the ATO recommends paying super on payday itself.

A business day is any day other than a Saturday, a Sunday, or a public holiday for the whole of any Australian state or territory. A state-wide holiday in WA counts even if you're in Brisbane. A holiday that covers only part of a state, like Royal Hobart Show Day, doesn't.

Worked example: you pay on a Friday

Say you pay your two staff every Friday. Start counting the next business day:

  • Monday (1), Tuesday (2), Wednesday (3), Thursday (4), Friday (5)
  • Monday (6), Tuesday (7)

Their super needs to be in their funds by the Tuesday after next, 11 days after payday. For each state-wide public holiday anywhere in Australia that falls in that stretch, the deadline moves out by one business day. The ATO's own examples match this: a Monday 6 July 2026 payday was due Wednesday 15 July, and super for a 10 December 2026 payday is due 21 December 2026.

Pay super on the Friday with the wages and you have the whole window to fix a rejected payment.

What counts as qualifying earnings

Qualifying earnings is the new base for working out super. The ATO says it includes "ordinary time earnings, all commissions, salary sacrifice contributions and other amounts that were previously included in an employee's salary or wages for super guarantee" (ATO). One real change: commissions for work done entirely outside ordinary hours are now included (ATO qualifying earnings).

You still pay super for the same people, including contractors paid mainly for their labour. Your payroll now reports qualifying earnings and super liability through Single Touch Payroll each payday.

What happened to the quarterly dates

The old due dates were 28 October, 28 January, 28 April and 28 July. The April to June 2026 quarter was the last one under the old rules, due in employees' funds by 28 July 2026. If that was missed, the ATO says you must lodge a super guarantee charge statement by 28 August and pay the charge for the quarter, and the late payment offset isn't available. Money received on or after 29 July gets applied under Payday Super, even if you meant it for the June quarter (ATO changeover guide).

The ATO clearing house has closed

The Small Business Superannuation Clearing House closed to new users on 1 October 2025 and closed permanently on 1 July 2026. Logins stopped at 11:59 pm AEST on 30 June 2026, and there's no read-only access to old records (ATO). If you used it, the ATO suggests checking your payroll software first, since it may already pay super, then super funds, commercial clearing houses or other payroll providers.

If you're late: the new super guarantee charge

For paydays from 1 July 2026 you don't lodge a super guarantee statement. The ATO calculates the charge for each payday and sends you a notice of assessment. The charge has four parts (ATO):

  • the unpaid super for each employee
  • notional earnings, which is interest at the general interest charge rate, compounding daily
  • an administrative uplift, starting at 60% of the unpaid super plus notional earnings
  • a choice loading of 25% if you didn't follow the choice of fund rules, capped at $1,200 per notice period

The uplift can come down, as far as 0%, if you haven't had an ATO-initiated assessment in the 2 years before that payday and you lodge a voluntary disclosure quickly. The new charge is tax deductible; the old quarterly one wasn't. If you realise you've missed a payment, the ATO's advice is to pay the right amount to the fund as soon as you notice, as long as you haven't already been assessed.

For the first year (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027) the ATO says it won't review employers who pay super for each payday and fix errors quickly. It's focusing on employers who aren't trying to change over, aren't fixing errors, or aren't paying at all (ATO compliance approach).

Checklist for a small employer

  • Payroll software works out super on qualifying earnings and reports qualifying earnings and super liability through STP
  • You're off the old ATO clearing house and know exactly how your super gets paid now
  • You know how long your payroll provider or clearing house takes to get money to funds, and whether it uses the New Payments Platform (the ATO says this can land the same day)
  • Super goes out on payday, in the same sitting as wages
  • You know where rejection messages show up (payroll, clearing house portal or the fund) and check after each pay run
  • Fund details, member numbers and USIs are right for every employee; use a member verification request if your software offers one
  • New starters get a choice of fund form; if they don't choose, request their stapled fund from the ATO (since 27 March 2026 you can offer it with the choice form). Their first contribution has 20 business days
  • Bonuses or back pay outside the normal pay cycle: the super is due by the same date as the super for the next regular payday
  • Cash flow: super now leaves the account every pay run, so set it aside on payday instead of letting it pile up
  • State-wide public holidays around Christmas and Easter are marked, because they push due dates out
