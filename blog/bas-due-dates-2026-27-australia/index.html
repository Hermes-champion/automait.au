BAS due dates 2026–27 for Australian sole traders

By AUTOMAIT · 11 November 2026

If you're a sole trader, the date that matters most is usually the 28th of the month after each quarter ends, with one big exception: the December quarter is due 28 February.

Dates checked on the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) website on 11 November 2026. Your own BAS shows your due date, so treat it as the final word (ATO).

General information only, not tax advice. For your situation, talk to a registered tax or BAS agent or contact the ATO.

Quarterly BAS due dates

You lodge quarterly if your GST turnover is under $20 million and the ATO hasn't told you to report monthly. The four standard dates are 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July (ATO, ATO quarterly GST).

Quarter 2 gets an extra month built in. The ATO says a later due date "does not apply for quarter 2 because the due date already includes a one-month extension" (ATO).

When a due date lands on a weekend or public holiday

"If the due date is on a weekend or public holiday, you have until the next business day to lodge and pay" (ATO). The ATO counts a public holiday that applies to the whole of any state or territory, and in that case taxpayers in all states and territories can use the next business day (ATO).

That's why the December quarter BAS for 2026–27 moves twice. 28 February 2027 is a Sunday. Monday 1 March 2027 is Labour Day in WA, which the ATO's table shows with a first business day of Tuesday 2 March (ATO).

Extra time if you lodge online yourself

If you receive and lodge your quarterly BAS online, you may get an extra 2 weeks to lodge and pay for quarters 1, 3 and 4. If you qualify, the due date on your BAS updates automatically. It doesn't apply to monthly BAS or to quarterly instalment notices (ATO 2-week concession terms).

Extra time through a tax or BAS agent

Registered agents have a lodgment program with later dates for eligible clients (see the table). The agent must lodge electronically; paper lodgment, monthly GST cycles and instalment notices (forms R, S and T) are excluded (ATO). Ask your agent which date applies to you (ATO).

Monthly BAS

A monthly BAS is due on the 21st of the following month, so a July BAS is due 21 August (ATO). It's compulsory at $20 million GST turnover or if the ATO directs you. One exception on the ATO's agent lodgment program page: the December monthly BAS is due 21 February for businesses with up to $10 million turnover that report GST monthly and lodge electronically (ATO).

Annual GST reporting

If you registered for GST voluntarily (GST turnover under $75,000), you can elect to report and pay GST once a year. You elect by 28 October if you currently report quarterly, or by 21 August if monthly. The annual GST return is due on your income tax return due date, or 28 February after the year ends if you don't have to lodge a tax return (ATO annual GST reporting). For self-lodgers that's 31 October (ATO).

Not registered for GST? PAYG instalments

You must register for GST once your GST turnover reaches $75,000, and within 21 days of crossing the threshold (ATO). Below that, there's no GST to report, but you may still owe tax during the year through pay as you go (PAYG) instalments.

You're automatically entered into PAYG instalments when your latest tax return shows instalment income of $4,000 or more, tax payable of $1,000 or more and estimated (notional) tax of $500 or more. You can also join voluntarily. Most people pay quarterly (ATO).

  • Dates: quarterly instalments are due 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July (ATO).
  • Notice instead of a BAS: if you pay the ATO's instalment amount and have no other reporting, you get an instalment notice (ATO). Pay it by the due date; no need to lodge it (ATO).
  • No 2-week online extension for instalment notices (ATO).
  • Annual option: some people can pay one annual instalment instead if they meet the ATO's conditions, which include notional tax under $8,000 and not being required to register for GST (ATO).

What happens if you're late

If you can't lodge or pay on time, contact the ATO or your tax professional before the due date (ATO). Payment plans may be available.

  • Failure to lodge penalty: one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part) late, up to 5 penalty units for individuals and small withholders (ATO). A penalty unit is $364 for late lodgments on or after 1 July 2026 (ATO). The ATO generally doesn't penalise isolated late lodgments and warns you first.
  • General interest charge (GIC): may apply to amounts unpaid after the due date, and compounds daily. GIC incurred on or after 1 July 2025 can't be claimed as a tax deduction (ATO).

Frequently asked questions

When is the December quarter BAS due for 2026–27?

The quarter 2 BAS (October to December 2026) has a standard due date of 28 February 2027. That's a Sunday, and Monday 1 March 2027 is Labour Day in WA, so under the ATO's next-business-day rule the ATO's public holiday table shows Tuesday 2 March 2027 as the first business day. Check the due date on your BAS, and don't leave it to the last day.

Do tax agents get extra time to lodge BAS?

Yes. Registered tax and BAS agents have a lodgment program with later due dates for eligible clients who lodge electronically. For 2026–27 the ATO lists 25 November 2026 (Q1), 26 May 2027 (Q3) and 25 August 2027 (Q4). There's no agent extension for Q2, which is already due 28 February.

Do I need to lodge a BAS if I'm not registered for GST?

Not for GST. You must register once your GST turnover reaches $75,000. Without GST registration you may still have PAYG instalments, and if paying the ATO's instalment amount is your only obligation, you get a quarterly instalment notice instead of a BAS and just pay it by the due date.

What are the PAYG instalment due dates for sole traders?

Quarterly PAYG instalments follow the same dates as quarterly BAS: 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July. Instalment notices don't get the 2-week online lodgment extension.

What happens if I lodge my BAS late?

The ATO may apply a failure to lodge penalty of one penalty unit ($364 from 1 July 2026) for every 28 days (or part) it's late, up to 5 units for individuals and small withholders. It generally warns you first. Unpaid amounts may also attract the general interest charge.
