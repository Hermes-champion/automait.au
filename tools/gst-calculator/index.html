How the calculator works

  • Add GST: GST = amount before GST × 10%; total = amount + GST
  • Remove GST: GST = GST-inclusive price ÷ 11; amount before GST = price − GST
  • Quote total: subtotal = sum of items; if registered, GST = subtotal × 10%
  • GST is rounded to the nearest cent (half a cent rounds up)

ExampleExample amounts only.

  • Add GST to $1,000: GST is $100, total $1,100.
  • Remove GST from $1,100: $1,100 ÷ 11 = $100 GST, so $1,000 before GST.
  • Remove GST from $99.99: $99.99 ÷ 11 = $9.0900…, so GST is $9.09 and the amount before GST is $90.90.
  • Add GST to $99.99: 10% is $9.999, which rounds to $10.00, so the total is $109.99.

GST is 10%, but you divide by 11 to take it out

The ATO describes GST as a tax of 10% on most goods, services and other items sold or consumed in Australia. The 10% is worked out on the price before GST.

So when a price already includes GST, the GST is one-eleventh of it, not 10% of it. A $1,100 price is $1,000 plus $100 GST, and $1,100 ÷ 11 = $100. Taking 10% of $1,100 would give $110, which is wrong. The ATO's tax invoices page uses the same idea: an invoice can say 'Total price includes GST' when the GST is exactly one-eleventh of the total price.

The same page sets out how to round: to the nearest cent, rounding half a cent up. For an invoice with several taxable items, one of the methods it describes is to add up the ex-GST amounts and work out GST once on that total, which is what the quote total above does.

Do you need to register for GST?

As a general rule, the ATO says you must register for GST within 21 days once your GST turnover (gross business income, not counting GST) reaches $75,000 or more, or when you expect it to, for example in your first year of a new business. Below that, registering is optional, unless you want to claim fuel tax credits, which needs GST registration.

There's also an exception for drivers: if you provide taxi or limousine travel for passengers, including ride-sourcing (rideshare), the ATO says you must register for GST regardless of your turnover.

Check the details for your situation on the ATO's Registering for GST page.

If you're not registered for GST

  • Don't charge GST. The ATO notes that a supplier who isn't registered or required to be registered for GST can't charge GST.
  • Don't show a GST amount on your quotes or invoices.
  • Don't call your invoices 'tax invoices'. The ATO says if you're not registered, your invoices should not include the words 'tax invoice'. Use a standard invoice instead.

In the quote total above, leave "I'm registered for GST" switched off and the total has no GST in it.

If your GST turnover reaches $75,000 (or you expect it to), register within 21 days. The ATO says that if you don't register when you're required to, you may have to pay GST on sales made since then, even if you didn't include GST in the price.

Not everything has GST

Some sales are GST-free. The ATO's GST-free sales page lists examples including most basic foods, some education courses and some medical, health and care products and services. If you're registered and some of what you sell is GST-free, don't add GST to those items. The quote total above assumes every item is taxable.

Common questions

How do I work out the GST in a price that already includes GST?

Divide the GST-inclusive price by 11. For $1,100, the GST is $1,100 ÷ 11 = $100, and the price before GST is $1,000.

This works when the whole price is taxable at the standard 10% rate.

Why can't I just take 10% off the total?

Because GST is 10% of the price before GST, not 10% of the total. The GST in $1,100 is $100, but 10% of $1,100 is $110. Taking 10% off would leave $990, which is $10 short of the real ex-GST price of $1,000.

How is GST rounded?

To the nearest cent, with half a cent rounding up. That's the rule the ATO gives on its tax invoices page for an invoice with one taxable sale.

For a quote with several items, this calculator adds up the ex-GST amounts and works out GST once on the subtotal. That's one of the methods the ATO describes for invoices where every item is taxable.

I'm not registered for GST. Can I add GST to my prices?

No. The ATO says a business that isn't registered for GST (or required to be) can't charge GST. Don't add it to your quotes or invoices, and don't call your invoices 'tax invoices'.

Does every sale include GST?

No. Some sales are GST-free. The ATO gives most basic foods, some education courses and some medical, health and care products and services as examples. If you're registered, check whether what you sell is GST-free before adding GST.
