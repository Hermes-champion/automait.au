How the calculator works

  • Super set-aside = income × super %
  • Yearly amount to cover = income + super + running costs
  • Billable hours per year = weeks worked × billable hours per week
  • Break-even rate = yearly amount to cover ÷ billable hours per year
  • Rate to charge = break-even rate × (1 + buffer %)
  • Day rate = hourly rate × 8 hours
  • If you're registered for GST: rate to quote = rate × 1.1

The break-even rate is the least you can charge per billable hour and still cover the figures you entered, if you bill every one of those hours. The buffer sits on top of that.

Why your billable hours are fewer than the hours you work

Only the hours you can put on an invoice pay for the year. A lot of a tradie's working week can't be charged to anyone, for example:

  • quoting, site visits and measure-ups for jobs you don't win
  • driving between jobs and picking up materials
  • invoicing, chasing payments, bookkeeping and BAS
  • callbacks and fixing problems at no charge
  • answering calls, messages and emails

Weeks work the same way. You won't be on the tools all 52 weeks once you take holidays, public holidays, sick days and quiet patches between jobs into account.

ExampleExample figures only, not averages.

  • Weeks: 52 weeks, minus 4 weeks' holiday and 2 weeks for public holidays and sick days, is 46 weeks.
  • Hours: 40 hours a week working, minus 10 hours of quoting, travel and admin, is 30 billable hours.

A worked example

ExampleThese are the example figures filled into the calculator, not real or typical figures.

  1. Income wanted: $80,000 a year before tax. Super at 12%: $9,600. Running costs: $20,000.
  2. Yearly amount to cover: $80,000 + $9,600 + $20,000 = $109,600.
  3. Billable hours: 46 weeks × 30 hours = 1,380 hours.
  4. Break-even rate: $109,600 ÷ 1,380 = $79.42 an hour.
  5. With a 15% buffer: $79.42 × 1.15 = $91.33 an hour, or $730.67 for an 8-hour day.
  6. Registered for GST? Add 10%: $100.47 an hour to quote.

Now say you'd worked the rate out as if every hour were billable, at 48 weeks × 40 hours = 1,920 hours. You'd get $109,600 ÷ 1,920 = $57.08 an hour. If you then bill the 1,380 hours from the example, that rate brings in about $78,775, which is $30,825 short of the $109,600 you needed.

What this rate doesn't include

  • Income tax. Your income figure is before tax, so tax comes out of it. The calculator doesn't work out tax. This isn't tax advice; check ato.gov.au or a registered tax agent.
  • Materials for each job. These usually go on the quote separately, with your markup. Try the markup vs margin calculator.
  • GST. The rate is ex GST unless you switch on GST. GST you collect isn't income you keep.

Super if you're a sole trader

The ATO says self-employed sole traders and partners don't have to pay super guarantee for themselves, but can choose to make personal super contributions.

The calculator starts at 12% because that's the super guarantee rate employers pay for employees from 1 July 2025. It's there so you can budget for super if you want to. Enter 0 to leave it out.

When to add GST

Only add GST if you're registered for it. If you are, work out your rate ex GST first, then add 10%. The GST you collect goes to the ATO, so it isn't part of your income.

As a general rule, the ATO says you must register for GST within 21 days once your GST turnover (gross business income, not counting GST) reaches $75,000 or more, or when you expect it to, for example in your first year of a new business. Below that, registering is optional, unless you want to claim fuel tax credits, which needs GST registration. Check the details for your situation on the ATO's Registering for GST page.

Common questions

Does this calculator work out my income tax?

No. The income figure is what you want to earn before tax, so your income tax comes out of it. The calculator doesn't work out tax, Medicare levy or anything you set aside for it.

This isn't tax advice. For tax, check ato.gov.au or talk to a registered tax agent.

Do I have to pay myself super as a sole trader?

The ATO says self-employed sole traders and partners don't have to pay super guarantee for themselves, but can choose to make personal super contributions.

The 12% filled in is the super guarantee rate employers pay for employees from 1 July 2025. It's there so you can budget for super if you want to. Set it to 0 to leave super out.

Should I add GST to my hourly rate?

Only if you're registered for GST. If you are, add 10% GST on top of your rate when you quote and invoice. Switch on “I'm registered for GST” and the calculator shows the rate with GST added.

If you're not registered, don't add GST to your quotes or invoices.

What counts as business running costs?

Costs of running the business across the year that aren't tied to one job: for example vehicle costs, insurance, tools and equipment, phone, software and subscriptions, licences and accounting fees.

Materials for a particular job are usually charged on that job's quote instead. Our markup vs margin calculator can help price those.

What is the buffer for?

The break-even rate only covers the figures you entered. A buffer gives you room for things that are hard to plan: quiet weeks, jobs that run over, invoices paid late or not at all, and costs that go up during the year.

15% is just a starting figure, not a recommended profit margin. Change it to suit your business.
